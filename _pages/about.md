## Highlighted Projects

### Line ratios in the extended outer filament of Centaurus A
##### Supervised by: Steven Tingay, ICRAR Curtin 
##### Deliverables: Publication (submitted to PASA)
###### Summary:

<p>Centaurus A, one of the brightest radio sources in the sky and a nearby galaxy, has extended jetted structure on the order of tens of kiloparsecs northward from its galactic nucleus. Our collaborator, Rolf Olsen, used his self-constructed telescope to acquire spectacular optical images. We flux calibrated his images and used it to study line enrichment in the most extended structures of the outermost optical features. This region is interesting, and possibly a great target for follow-up IFU spectroscopy, due to the proximate and spatially anti-coincident X-ray and radio emission. The paper has been submitted to PASA and we are waiting for the first round of reviews. </p>

### Molecular gas in main sequence cosmic noon galaxies
##### Supervised by: Chelsea Sharon, Clackamas Community College, Yale-NUS College (formerly)
##### Deliverables: Publication in preparation, conference posters, talk and proposals to ALMA (Co-I; 2023.1.01619.S)
###### Summary:

The epoch of z ~ 2 is called cosmic noon, which coincides with the highest observed rates of star formation in the universe. I studied the interplay between molecular gas and star formation rate. More specifically, I used existing VLA, ALMA and *HST* observations of strongly lensed galaxies to constrain the spatially resolved Schmidt-Kennicutt (SK) relation. Apart from one of the galaxies in our sample, the Cosmic Eye, the other sources were non-detections in the VLA CO(1–0) detections. Consequently, to see if any interesting constraints were possible despite the non-detections, I created mockup observations using CASA. Based on these simulations, we find that a couple of our sources are offset to higher star formation efficiencies than previously thought, due to their constrained placements on the SK relation. We are in the final stages of preparing a draft (you can find the manuscript PDF attached) and we plan to submit to ApJ once we've addressed feedback from collaborators.
